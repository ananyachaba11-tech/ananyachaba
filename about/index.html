Some data-stuff I have learnt along the way:

I have collected primary data, designed surveys, built methodologies for data models, and worked with public systems on the ground. It has taught me to treat a dataset as a source, not the source of truth.

I have spent a lot of time cleaning messy datasets. It has taught me the patience to trace inconsistencies, make careful decisions, and document those decisions along the way.

I have worked with statistical and machine-learning models. It has taught me that the analytical approach should follow the question, not the other way around.

More recently, I have been using code to find and build datasets where a neat, downloadable one does not exist. It has taught me that data can sometimes be found where it seemingly does not exist — and I find that particularly thrilling!

And once I have something worth saying, I spend time thinking about how best to communicate what I find. It has taught me that how information is presented affects how easily it is absorbed and what a reader takes away from it.

And across all of this, I try to hold myself to high standards of methodological rigour, integrity, and transparency. I believe that good data work is as much about recognising what you cannot claim as it is about finding what you can.
